Meet Tall Boy, a hybrid humanoid designed to take on everyday warehouse work.

A wheeled hybrid humanoid capable of automating any mundane task in a warehouse or a factory. Designed to slot into existing layouts, Tall Boy moves, sees, and manipulates.

What the robot can do — capabilities & skills

Robot Capabilities
  • •Pick items directly from shelves.
  • •Pick and place items from totes.
  • •Cardboard folding and packaging.
  • •Label sticking on boxes and pouches.
  • •Scan barcodes, read expiry dates, and check product quality.
  • •Sort items by type, quality, or other properties.
  • •Restock items neatly on shelves.
  • •Restock items inside totes.
  • •Operate semi-automated fillers, packing machines, and touch-screen workflows.
  • •Perform simple assembly tasks like plugging connectors.

Technology stack that makes it possible

1. The AI Brain (VLA Models)

Robots are powered by foundation models like NVIDIA's GR00T. These Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models translate natural language commands directly into physical actions.

2. Digital Twin Training

Before deployment, robots train for thousands of hours in photorealistic simulators like Isaac Sim. This allows for rapid, safe, and cost-effective learning in a virtual environment.

3. Sim-to-Real Transfer

Techniques like Domain Randomization bridge the gap between simulation and reality, ensuring the AI can handle the unpredictability of the real world.

4. Learning by Imitation

Robots now learn complex tasks by watching humans. Data collected via teleoperation turns human demonstration into a powerful, code-free way to program.

5. Advanced Perception

Using 3D depth cameras and AI like SAM2 (Segment Anything Model 2), robots can instantly identify and isolate any object, giving them superhuman scene understanding.

6. The Software Backbone

ROS 2 (Robot Operating System 2) is the industry-standard software framework, with NVIDIA Isaac providing GPU-accelerated libraries for high-speed computation.

7. Building a World Memory

World Foundation Models like NVIDIA Cosmos give robots a persistent 4D memory of their environment, allowing them to understand object permanence and context.

8. AI-Generated Motion

Diffusion models, famous for creating AI art, now generate smooth and efficient robot movements, replacing rigid, traditionally programmed paths.

9. Smarter, Cheaper Hardware

Powerful onboard computers like NVIDIA Jetson Thor run the AI, while software intelligence enables the use of cheaper arms by compensating for mechanical imperfections.

10. The Data Flywheel

Data from deployed robots continuously refines the core AI models. This creates a powerful feedback loop where the entire fleet of robots gets smarter over time.
